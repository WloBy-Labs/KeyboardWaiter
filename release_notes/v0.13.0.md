# KeyboardWaiter 0.13.0

A habitat now sits on your desktop, and species move into it based on how you actually use
this Mac.

## Field guide and habitat

- **Species are discovered, not levelled up.** Each one has a condition drawn from real usage —
  a long night, a day spent inside one app, a heavy delete-key day. The condition stays hidden
  until the species shows up; that reveal is the point.
- When you get close (85%), the guide tells you the *direction* — "something about when you
  work" — without giving away the condition itself.
- Discovered species live in a small habitat window in the corner of the screen. Ones you have
  kept doing lately are lively; ones you have drifted away from go dormant.
- Ten species ship in this release. Adding more is a rule plus a sprite, by design.

## Pluggable module

The whole pet system is a separate SwiftPM target (`KeyboardWaiterPet`) that depends on the app
core, never the other way round. The core exposes one `AppFeature` extension point and does not
know pets exist; assembly happens in `main.swift`. Deleting the directory, the target, and three
lines of assembly leaves a working app — verified by actually doing it.

Turning the pet off in the menu stops all of it: no window, no evaluation, no work.

## Notes

- Growth counts only from the day you adopt. A months-old install does not unlock the whole
  guide on the first launch.
- Days recorded before 0.12.0 have no app attribution, so app-based species skip them rather
  than reading them as "one app all day".
- `scripts/install_local.sh` is the supported way to install a local build: it reuses the
  signing identity from `signing.env` and updates the bundle in place, so the Input Monitoring
  grant survives. Switching signing identities or deleting the .app is what makes macOS ask
  again.
